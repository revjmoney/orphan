READ THIS FIRST

3.3 V only. The FPGA pins are not 5 V tolerant. A 5 V bus needs a level shifter (an I2C one, such as a PCA9306 or a BSS138 pair).

Connect SCL, SDA and GND. Do not connect the S9's 3.3 V to a powered target. The bus needs pull-ups: the S9's own I2C bus has 1 kohm pull-ups on the board; on header pins MIMIK only has the FPGA's weak internal pull-ups, so the target bus must have its own (2.2 to 4.7 kohm to 3.3 V).

Header pin numbers: the 2 x 9 hashboard connectors J1..J9. Per Ardzy's tested table: GND = pins 1, 2, 9, 10, 13, 14, 3.3 V = pin 16, FPGA pins 5, 11, 12, 15. The board's I2C bus is on pin 3 (SDA) and pin 4 (SCL) of J1..J8 (Ardzy's guide; confirm with a meter on your board before the first connection).

To stand in for a chip on a board: remove (or lift the SCL / SDA legs of) the original chip, or keep it and give MIMIK a different address. Two chips on one address fight.

HOW IT WORKS

The FPGA answers the bus master at the chip's own speed from the chip's registers. Twenty times a second the board turns the values here into the exact register bytes of that chip (its units, byte order, gain settings and checksums) and puts them in between two bus accesses, so a master never reads half of a new value.

fixed holds the slider value. sine, ramp, square and noise move around it by the amplitude, once per period: a fake temperature drifting, a robot that turns, a distance that jumps.

Read-only registers (measurements, IDs) keep their value when the target writes to them, like the real chip; LIVE shows such writes as BLOCKED.
